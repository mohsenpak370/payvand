A blockchain has more than one version. The real one is called **mainnet**, and real money lives
there. There is also a practice version, **devnet**, where the same software runs with tokens that
cost nothing and are worth nothing.

Payvand can run on either. When the header shows a **devnet** badge, everything on the screen is
practice: the dollars are free test dollars, the euros are free test euros, and the point is to press
every button without risking a cent.

## Where practice money comes from

A **faucet** — a website that hands out test tokens for free:

- **SOL**, which pays the tiny network fees: `solana.com/faucet`
- **USDC and EURC**: `faucet.circle.com`

Send the practice tokens to the address of the wallet you are testing with, and it behaves exactly
like a real balance.

## Why devnet exists

The motions are the same as real money: setting a limit, approving it once, paying an invoice,
refilling the pot, closing the budget and watching the money come back. Learning those motions with
tokens that do not matter is the difference between a calm first week and an expensive lesson.

It is also where you discover the small things nobody explains: that a wallet needs a little SOL to
move anything, that approving a limit moves money out of the wallet, and that closing the budget
returns what is left.

## The one mistake to avoid

Devnet and mainnet look identical from the outside — same kind of address, same apps, same wallet.
They are separate worlds:

- **Test tokens can never be sold or swapped for real money.** Anything offering to do so is a scam.
- **Never send real money to an address you were given for testing**, expecting it to come back. It
  will not: on mainnet that address is just somebody's address.
- Conversely, real money sent on mainnet does not appear in the devnet app, and practice money sent
  on devnet is worthless on mainnet.

## What this means for you

If this is your first time, use devnet and treat it as a flight simulator. Set a small limit, pay
something, refill it, close it, look at what came back. Then, when you switch to real money, nothing
on the screen will be new — only the numbers will be.
