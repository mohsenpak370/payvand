When you set up a wallet it shows you twelve or twenty-four words — usually called a recovery
phrase or seed phrase. Those words generate every private key the wallet will ever use. Anyone
who reads them can move everything, from any device, without your phone and without your
password.

That single fact explains almost every rule below.

## The rules that matter

**Write them on paper, by hand, and keep the paper somewhere deliberate.** A drawer at home and a
second copy at another address is plenty for most people. Do not rely on memory.

**Never type them into a website.** No legitimate site needs them. A page that asks is a phishing
page, even if its address looks right and even if you got there from a link someone you trust
sent.

**Never photograph them, and never put them in cloud notes.** Photos sync to accounts, accounts
get breached, and a screenshot in your camera roll is readable by anything with access to your
phone. The same goes for email drafts, chat messages to yourself, and password managers that sync
to the cloud.

**Never read them to anyone.** Not support, not a "wallet validator", not a giveaway organiser,
not an exchange employee, not a family member over the phone. No legitimate service has ever
needed them, and no legitimate service will ever ask.

**Beware the second wallet.** A common scam is to be told to "move your funds to a safe wallet"
and given a phrase to restore. The phrase is the scammer's, so the funds land in their wallet.

## What losing the phrase actually costs

If you lose the phrase and still have the phone, you can keep using the wallet — until the phone
is lost, broken, wiped or the app is reinstalled. At that moment the balance is unreachable by
anyone, forever. There is no recovery team, because there is no account to recover.

The mirror image is just as important: if someone else obtains the phrase, they do not need to
steal your phone. They restore the wallet elsewhere and the balance leaves while your app still
sits there looking normal.

## Test the backup before it matters

A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup. Once, with a small balance:

1. Install the same wallet on a second device, or reset the first one after moving the balance to
   a fresh wallet.
2. Restore from the written phrase.
3. Confirm the address matches the one you wrote down.

If step 3 fails, you have found a transcription error while it is cheap.

## Going further

- **A hardware wallet** keeps the keys on a device that never touches the internet and shows the
  amount and recipient on its own screen, so a compromised computer cannot silently change a
  payment. It is the single biggest security upgrade for anyone holding meaningful amounts.
- **Separate wallets for separate jobs.** A "hot" wallet on your phone for daily amounts, and a
  wallet you only touch for savings. Losing the phone then costs you the till, not the safe.
- **Passphrase protection** (sometimes called the 25th word) adds a secret to the phrase. It helps
  against a stolen paper backup and hurts badly if you forget it, so only use it with a second,
  separate backup plan.

Payvand never asks for a recovery phrase, and it has no use for one: signing happens in your
wallet, and the app only ever receives a signature for the payment in front of you.
