Paying by wallet means a popup and a fingerprint check every time. That is fine once. It is
miserable at a coffee counter with a queue behind you.

The monthly allowance is the way out of that. You approve **one** payment a month, and every small
payment after that goes through with no prompt at all.

## What you are agreeing to

You choose a limit — say 50 USDC. When you approve it, two things happen:

1. **That amount moves out of your wallet** into a pot that belongs to your wallet. It is not sent to
   Payvand and not sent to any shop. Think of it as taking cash out of your account and putting it in
   your own coat pocket.
2. **The app is given a key** that lives on your device. That key can pay invoices from the pot, and
   nothing else.

## What the key can do — and cannot

It can pay a shop, up to the limit, this month.

It cannot send money to a stranger, cannot move the pot anywhere else, cannot exceed the limit, and
cannot touch the rest of your wallet. If someone stole the device, the most they could do is spend
what is left of this month's limit — and you would see it happen, because every payment is public.

That narrowness is the whole safety argument. It is not "the app can spend your money"; it is "the
app can pay invoices, with this month's pocket money, until it runs out".

## The buttons

- **Set / save limit** — the one approval. It moves the money into the pot, registers this device,
  and starts the month.
- **Refill** — puts the pot back to the full limit mid-month, for example when 20 is left of 50.
  Same single approval. A refill also restarts the month's counter, so the whole limit is spendable
  again.
- **Use this device instead** — if the allowance was set up on another phone or laptop, this takes
  it over in one approval and stops the old device being able to spend.
- **Close budget** — the kill switch. It returns everything left to your wallet and cancels the key
  everywhere.

## Why the limit should be small

The limit is the most a lost or stolen device could ever cost you, so a small limit is doing its job
rather than being a compromise. Pair it with a wallet used only for spending —
[here is how to split a spending wallet from your savings](/learn/two-wallets).

## If the phone is lost

Move to another device, open the budget page, and **close the budget** with your wallet: whatever is
left comes back, and the lost device's key stops working immediately. Then follow
[the lost-phone checklist](/learn/if-your-phone-is-lost).

## When to pay from your wallet instead

Use the allowance for the everyday: a coffee, a market stall, a regular supplier. Pay directly from
your wallet for a one-off, a larger amount, or a shop you have not dealt with before — then the
approval screen is in front of you, which is where you want it. Both ways pay the same invoices.
