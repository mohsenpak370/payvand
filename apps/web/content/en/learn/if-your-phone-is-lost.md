A lost phone is annoying. A lost phone with a wallet on it is a countdown, and the countdown is
shorter if the phone was unlocked or had no passcode.

Work through this in order. None of it takes long, and the order matters more than the speed.

## 1. Lock or erase the phone

Use **Find My** on an Apple device or **Find My Device** on Android to mark it lost and, if you
cannot get it back, erase it. Call your mobile carrier if the SIM is in it, and change the password on
the email account the phone was signed into — that email can often reset everything else.

## 2. Work out what was actually on it

There is a big difference between these two cases:

- **A wallet app** (Phantom, Solflare, Backpack, a hardware wallet companion). The phone may hold the
  keys, or at least the phrase you wrote down somewhere. Treat this as serious.
- **An app like Payvand**, which has no seed phrase and cannot move your money to itself. The worst
  case is the remainder of this month's allowance —
  [explained here](/learn/monthly-allowance-explained).

## 3. A wallet app: assume the worst if it was unlocked

- **If the phone was locked with a passcode you trust**, and the phrase is safe at home: install the
  same wallet on a new phone, restore from the phrase, and carry on. Nothing is lost.
- **If it was unlocked, or the passcode was known, or the phrase was stored on the phone**: treat the
  wallet as compromised. On a clean device, create a **new** wallet with a **new** phrase and move
  anything left into it. Changing the app's password does not help — the keys are what the thief
  would have, not the password.
- If the old wallet only held a small spending balance, the calm version is: create a new spending
  wallet, note the loss, and be glad it was the till and not the safe
  ([why that is the point of two wallets](/learn/two-wallets)).

## 4. An allowance: close it from another device

Open the budget page on another device, connect the wallet, and press **Close budget**. Your wallet
signature returns everything left in the pot, and the key on the lost phone stops working instantly.
If you cannot do that, the exposure is capped at the limit you set, for the rest of the month.

## 5. Do not fall for the follow-up scam

Within hours of losing a wallet, people are often contacted by someone offering to "recover" the
funds for a fee, or asking for the recovery phrase "to secure it". Both are the same theft, twice.
Nobody legitimate can recover a wallet, and no support person ever needs your phrase. See
[how to spot a crypto scam](/learn/spotting-crypto-scams).

## 6. Afterwards

Report the theft to the police and, if the loss was a scam, to your local fraud reporting service.
Then change the habit that made it expensive: a passcode on the phone, the phrase on paper at home
rather than in the phone, and only a small balance in the wallet you carry.

## What this means for you

The phone is replaceable and so is a spending wallet. What is not replaceable is a phrase you never
wrote down, or savings sitting in the wallet you carry to the shop. Both problems are solved before
the phone is lost, not after.
