Money in a bank is a number in the bank's own database. You cannot open that database, and you
cannot change the number yourself. You ask the bank, and the bank does it.

Crypto turns that around. The record of who owns what is kept by thousands of computers at the
same time, anyone may read it, and no single company owns it. Moving money is something you do
with a key only you hold — not something you request and wait for approval on.

That is the whole idea. The coins, the apps and the QR codes all follow from it.

## Why the name "crypto"

Crypto is short for **cryptography**, which is the mathematics of locks. Your wallet holds a secret
number, and that secret can produce a **signature** for a payment. A signature is proof that the
owner of the money agreed to that exact payment, without revealing the secret itself.

Think of a wax seal that cannot be peeled off one letter and stuck onto another. The shop can check
the seal; nobody can copy it.

## What actually moves

Two things, in practice:

- **The network's own coin.** On Solana it is called SOL. It is used to pay the tiny fee for every
  payment, and some people also hold it as an investment.
- **Tokens that sit on top of it.** USDC and EURC are tokens worth one dollar and one euro each,
  issued by a company called Circle. They are what most shops would rather be paid in, because the
  price does not jump around while you are at the counter. There is a
  [whole guide on stablecoins](/learn/stablecoins-explained).

## What is better about it

- **No application, no approval, no opening hours.** Nobody asks for your ID, your address or your
  salary, and nobody can freeze your balance because of where you were born.
- **The shop is paid directly.** No card network in the middle, no waiting two days for the money
  to arrive, no monthly terminal rental.
- **It works the same everywhere.** A payment to the next street and a payment to another country
  cost the same fraction of a cent.
- **Payments are final.** Once the network has written it down, nobody can take it back. Shops love
  this. As a buyer, it is also the part to be careful with.

## What it asks of you in return

- **There is no password reset.** Nobody holds a spare copy of your key, so nobody can restore it
  for you. The twelve words your wallet gives you are the whole account —
  [keep them properly](/learn/protecting-your-seed-phrase).
- **Mistakes are final.** Send to the wrong address and the money is gone. There is no chargeback
  and no support line to call.
- **You are the bank.** That is the point, and also the job. The safety guides exist because of it.

One thing crypto is *not*: anonymous. An address is not your name, but every payment that address
makes is visible to anyone who looks, forever.

## What this means for you

Treat crypto like the cash in your pocket, not like the savings in your bank. Keep a small amount
for paying, keep the rest somewhere deliberate, and learn the two or three habits that prevent
almost every loss. Start with [what a blockchain actually is](/learn/what-is-a-blockchain), then
[what a wallet does](/learn/what-is-a-crypto-wallet).
